# Notch Integration experiment

Enable **Settings → Experimental → Notch Integration** on a MacBook with a physical notch. Preferences live in `notch-integration.json` under Astra's user-data directory and default to disabled, Hidden resting view, hover enabled, track-change popups enabled, and fullscreen enabled.

Persistent resting content matches the detected camera-gap width and stays 24 points tall beneath the camera. A proximity peek grows a compact rounded wrap from inside the hardware outline and holds it; an intentional approach opens that same rounded outline directly into a preview 64 points wider than the notch. The revealed preview is 32 points tall beneath the camera, with a single centered title/artist line and the whole strip clickable. Track popups use that larger preview too. The visible contour and native click target grow together. The expanded player uses 440 × 174 points beneath the camera, with 92-point artwork beside system-font metadata, unboxed playback controls, and a filled seek bar with elapsed/remaining time. Mute, volume, and Open Astra share a quiet footer; collapse sits at the upper right. The backing window retains 224 points of depth for animation, with unused space passing clicks through.

Pointer expansion focuses the player container without drawing an initial ring around Play. Keyboard or accessibility activation focuses Play (or Open Astra when empty), and controls retain visible keyboard focus. Mute uses the existing audio engine's mute state, preserving the selected volume.

While Astra's main window is focused and visible, automatic resting metadata/scopes and track-change popups are suppressed. Intentional proximity, hover preview, and clicked expansion remain available. Focusing the main window clears active and pending notifications; switching away restores the configured resting view without replaying tracks observed in the foreground. Hidden/minimized main windows do not suppress the notch. Focus observers follow main-window replacement and are removed on disable or disposal.

Seeking retains the submitted target until a new playback snapshot confirms it (with a two-second fallback if playback does not acknowledge). Closing and reopening the panel preserves that pending position. Pointer, keyboard, and accessibility edits commit once from a stored draft; blur never reads a potentially stale range value and sends it back to playback. A track change or canceled gesture discards unfinished input.

The approach zone extends up to 52 points sideways and 64 points downward from the camera's lower edge, tapering at the corners. It produces an immediate peek with no automatic reveal deadline: a cursor parked over nearby browser tabs can hold the peek indefinitely. Reveal requires at least four points of inward travel, arrival within 16 points of the hardware's lower edge, and a 50 ms confirmation. Continuing inward, including overshooting into the camera gap, does not restart or cancel that confirmation. Direction is assessed over six-point segments so small aiming corrections and shallow diagonal approaches work. A visible persistent strip accepts approaches across its full 24-point height. Clear sideways turns or retreats cancel pending approach intent.

The detected camera gap itself also accepts a direct arrival followed by a 150 ms pause, covering jumps that skip the approach zone entirely. Movement defers that fallback so lateral menu-bar crossings do not open it. Adjacent menu-bar areas remain excluded, and all hardware/menu-bar clicks still pass through. Animation-driven hit-test updates cannot initiate either path. Existing previews remain open while the cursor is over the hardware and retain their 12-point exit buffer and 200 ms grace below it; the visible lip remains clickable. Explicit dismissal suppresses both reveal paths until the cursor leaves.

Opening morphs a single solid contour from a shape concealed inside the camera outline. The peek grows uniformly in 90 ms into a rounded outline 5–7 points wider on each side and 2–3 points beneath the hardware. It holds until hover intent is confirmed, then opens directly over 280 ms. Width and height share one eased progress, keeping equal upper and lower widths throughout; there is no extra flared-corner stage after the peek. The visible lower lip accepts a deliberate click to expand; its content stays hidden. Collapse returns the same rounded outline into the hardware over 220 ms. No gradient is used. Text appears as the lower edge reaches its area, and interrupted transitions start at the last painted contour. Reduced motion uses a 90 ms opacity fade.

Expanded content stays at its original size and full opacity while the collapsing contour clips it away. Only within one strip-height of the compact view does it hand off to preview content. This handoff follows the current geometry, so reversing a collapse preserves both shape and content opacity. Departing player controls become inert and leave the accessibility tree immediately; their painted appearance does not extend their interaction lifetime.

The native panel uses `NSWindowAnimationBehaviorNone`. Electron's macOS focus-release path orders a window out and back in; AppKit's default panel dismissal animation otherwise fades a full-window snapshot over the renderer's retraction. Already unfocused panels skip this ordering operation. Open Astra releases panel focus before focusing the main window, publishing the resting state after the transfer.

The main-process `NotchController` owns the window, preferences, timing, playback snapshot, display lifecycle, and sender validation. `NotchInteraction` accepts an explicit clock for deterministic timing tests. The dedicated lazy `window=notch` renderer never imports the main application or its audio engine. The existing playback bridge drains visualization samples once, combines both consumers' demands, and distributes samples to the mini player and notch.

The isolated `macos_notch.node` addon uses AppKit's safe area and auxiliary screen regions. Its nonactivating panel joins Spaces without changing application activation policy. Local and global mouse observers report approach/inside/button/Space flags, quantized proximity, and a motion flag; they return other applications' events unchanged and install no keyboard hooks. The renderer and native mouse acceptance use the same sampled contour each frame, so clipped content and transparent margins pass clicks through. The decorative wrap in the menu-bar lane always passes mouse events through. `enableLargerThanScreen` is necessary to prevent a one-point menu-bar clamp on the tested MacBook.

The notch oscilloscope uses the main scope's sample-rate-normalized 2048-sample window (about 43–46 ms), 4096-sample pitch-lock warmup, and fractional native trigger. It draws every sample into a 20-point-tall Retina canvas. The usual 1.8× visual gain decreases only when peaks would clip, then recovers over 300 ms. Spectrum uses a fixed 4096-point FFT, interpolated logarithmic frequencies from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, peak-preserving bin reduction, and one point per physical pixel. Its 45 ms attack and 170 ms release run on animation frames independently of IPC chunk timing. Paused scopes draw a static baseline; hidden scopes unsubscribe and cancel drawing/smoothing.

## Development checks

Build the native targets for the installed Electron version, then run `npm run build`. The addon is included by the existing `native/build/Release/*.node` packaging rule.

```sh
node --experimental-strip-types --loader ./scripts/node/electron-test-loader.mjs --test src/main/services/notchState.test.ts src/main/services/notchPrefs.test.ts src/main/services/notchController.test.cjs src/shared/notchContour.test.ts src/shared/miniVisualizerDemand.test.ts src/types/miniPlayer.test.ts src/main/services/miniWindowPrefs.test.ts src/renderer/appShellIsolation.test.ts src/renderer/components/notch/notchVisualizer.test.ts src/renderer/components/notch/NotchScope.test.cjs src/renderer/components/notch/useNotchSeek.test.cjs
npm run typecheck
npm run build
npm run check:packaging-config
env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE ./node_modules/.bin/electron scripts/research/notch-seek.electron.cjs
env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE ./node_modules/.bin/electron scripts/research/notch-seek.electron.cjs --motion-check
env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE ./node_modules/.bin/electron scripts/research/notch.electron.cjs --native-motion-check
env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE ./node_modules/.bin/electron scripts/research/notch.electron.cjs
```

The last command opens a disposable fixture host for pointer, animation, fullscreen, and playback-command checks. A separate instance lock prevents duplicate fixtures from stacking notch windows. It saves screenshots in `/private/tmp/astra-notch-qa`. Add `--motion-trace` to sample the first proximity reveal frame by frame into `reveal-trace.json`. Add `--track-file /path/to/track.flac` to read real metadata/artwork into the silent fixtures; the fixture controls can also remove artwork or clear the track. Add `--app` to open the full Astra app with a fresh temporary profile; import a music folder there for real playback checks. Neither mode opens the normal user profile.

`--native-motion-check` automatically exercises the collapse control, Escape, and Open Astra through the real controller and AppKit panel. It records only the panel's screen region into `native-collapse.mov`, writes renderer traces alongside it, and closes the fixture. Screen recording permission is needed. Inspect the composited recording: `webContents.capturePage()` and an offscreen renderer cannot detect a native window dismissal animation.

For silent visualization of real music, the fixture accepts `--pcm-file /path/to/samples.f32` with 48 kHz mono f32le samples. This loops the samples through the native visualizer path without playing audio. The default sine fixture now preserves phase between chunks.

## Validation record

Intent regressions cover indefinite tab-bar pauses, lateral travel and stops, turning toward neighbouring controls before/after commitment, fresh inward movement, slow/diagonal approaches, jitter, stale history, persistent strips, and exit/reopen suppression. The native fixture confirms hardware-relative pointer coordinates, peek at tab-bar height, and click-through beneath the held lip. Only genuine nearby mouse movement feeds intent, including the final event before a stop; distant movement remains deduplicated.

Sensitivity regressions additionally cover overshooting from below into the hardware without resetting the 50 ms confirmation, direct arrivals, crossing the camera gap laterally, shallow diagonal/curved approaches, nearby short approaches, cumulative sideways movement, and dismissal suppression inside the hardware.

The native dismissal revision was visually verified frame by frame in a composited MacBook screen recording: the solid contour shrinks across the player without the previous full-window fade/blur. The native check passes collapse, Escape, and Open Astra, including panel visibility and focus transfer. The rebuilt addon reports native animation disabled. A focused controller regression covers repeated dismissal and already-transferred focus.

The hidden Chromium motion check covers expanded retraction into Hidden, Metadata, Oscilloscope, and Spectrum, with full-opacity content retained until the contour approaches strip size, a late preview handoff, and immediate control inactivation. It also checks continuity when reversing within the content handoff, and an emulated reduced-motion crossfade without geometry animation. Frame traces and a midpoint capture are written to the temporary fixture directory. All 53 focused tests, typecheck, and production build pass after this revision.

Foreground suppression has deterministic coverage for all resting views, manual peek/hover/expansion, notification cancellation during loading, and restoration without stale popups. Controller tests cover focus/blur, hide/show, minimize/restore, Open Astra, visualization gating, window replacement, and observer cleanup.

The seek regression check reproduced the original 8 → 30 → 8 UI snap-back under Chromium. The fixed renderer retains 30 through immediate collapse/reopen and a delayed snapshot, and pointer dragging and keyboard seeking each send exactly one command. The hidden Electron check uses silent playback fixtures, with no native notch panel or normal user profile. Five deterministic seek tests cover stale DOM values on blur, old snapshots, rapid seeks, timeouts, cancellation, track changes, accessibility, and cleanup.

The visualizer revision passes all 44 focused tests, typecheck, and production build. Regression coverage includes sample preservation, fractional triggering, scope peak headroom, 4096-point FFT selection, bass interpolation, narrow treble peaks, 60/120 Hz smoothing equivalence, and subscription/animation cleanup. Both modes were visually checked in Electron using decoded Patchwork samples: the scope retains loud peaks within its canvas, and the spectrum has a continuous bass curve at 4096. The notch renderer still reports no main-app or audio-engine resources loaded.

The compact expanded-player revision passes all 35 focused tests, typecheck, and production build. Live Electron checks with Patchwork metadata/artwork confirm a 440 × 174-point player below the 32-point camera gap, with the footer inside the visible contour. Verified unboxed pointer-open focus, visible Tab focus, play/pause and skip commands, keyboard seeking and volume adjustment, mute/unmute restoring the selected volume, long metadata truncation, missing artwork, empty playback, Escape, and Open Astra. This pass uses silent playback fixtures; the renderer still reports no main-app or audio-engine resources loaded.

All 35 focused tests, typecheck, native compilation, and production build pass after the contour revision. Live frame tracing confirms that the full reveal ends at a 249 × 56-point preview on the tested MacBook. Native acceptance clears after collapse, hover remains nonactivating, expansion and Open Astra work, and a duplicate fixture launch exits without creating another panel.

Packaging-config validation and an unsigned arm64 macOS directory bundle were verified during the initial integration; its copied `Contents/Resources/native/macos_notch.node` loaded under Electron 40.10.0. That bundle predates the contour revision; the current addon has been compiled and loaded in the live Electron fixture.

On an arm64 MacBook with a 1512 × 982-point built-in display at 2× scaling, verified native geometry and loading; no attachment gap; hover dwell and exit grace; nonactivating preview; expansion, seeking, volume, play/pause and skipping with fixtures; outside-click dismissal and click-through margins; Open Astra; missing artwork and long metadata; both native scope modes; fullscreen show/hide and return; and removal of native observers when disabled. The full app also loaded Patchwork artwork and played muted FLAC tracks while the mini spectrum and notch oscilloscope were enabled.

Physical sleep/wake, display reconnection/scaling changes, and x64 packaging remain hardware/release checks. Reduced-motion demand gating has controller coverage; its OS preference transition still needs a manual visual pass. Fullscreen suppression uses the active application's system presentation state; with multiple displays, disabling fullscreen visibility may also suppress the notch when the active fullscreen app is on another display.
